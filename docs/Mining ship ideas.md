The "Sifter" Class Mining Operation
1. The Nanobot Cloud (The "Mist")
Instead of heavy drills, your ship uses Molecular Dissociation Swarms.

The Process: The bots don’t just break rocks; they act as a biological-style digestive system. They latch onto debris from the shattered satellites and use resonant frequencies or localized heat to break atomic bonds.

The Sorting: Imagine the cloud having "chromatographic" zones. One layer of the swarm only collects Silicates, another pulls Iron, and a third hunts for Rare Earth elements.

Visual Impact: To an observer, the ship looks like it’s followed by a shimmering, multi-colored aurora that "eats" asteroids and leaves behind a trail of useless, purified dust.

2. The Ring System (The "Tailings")
In traditional mining, "tailings" are the waste left over. In your scenario, the ring system is literally the exhaust pipe of the operation.

Artificial Rings: As the ship processes a moon, it ejects the unwanted elements back into a precise orbital plane. Over time, the planet gains a beautiful, shimmering ring made entirely of "refined waste"—the bones of its former moons.

The Hazard: Navigating this is tricky. The ship has to stay ahead of its own waste stream to avoid "clogging" its sensors with its own discarded dust.

3. The Ship: The Harvester
Since the "tools" are microscopic, the ship itself doesn't need giant claws. It needs:

Magnetic Leitmotifs: Massive electromagnetic arrays to guide and "corral" the nanobot swarm back into the cargo hold once they are "full" of harvested material.

The Refinery Core: A massive centrifuge at the heart of the ship where the nanobots "vomit" their gathered molecules before being sent back out for another pass.

The Sci-Fi Conflict: What happens if the nanobot swarm loses its "return" programming? You’d have an invisible, expanding cloud of grey goo that slowly turns everything in the star system into a fine, sorted powder.
